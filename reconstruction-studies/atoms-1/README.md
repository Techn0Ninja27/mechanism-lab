# atoms-1: subset experts with mask-dependent availability pooling

## Question and mechanism

The proposal trains 40 HistGradientBoostingClassifier experts, each on the rows where a fixed feature subset is observed. Each subset contains columns 0 and 1 plus 6–12 randomly selected columns from 2–19. Each expert sees only values in that subset; no missingness indicators are passed to its estimator. At inference, an expert contributes only when all columns in its subset are observed, and its logit is averaged with the other available experts' logits. The mean is temperature-scaled using validation labels. Validation loss weights are also estimated from the validation cases, with weights frozen before final evaluation.

There is a material mechanism caveat. Although expert inputs omit indicators, the set of experts averaged is a deterministic function of the row's mask. Thus the overall predictor still has a mask-to-output path. Averaging logits from different experts does not algebraically cancel this dependence in general. Under own-value MNAR, availability patterns can be informative about withheld values and Y; an expert's prediction conditional on its subset being observed need not equal its unconditional E[Y|X_S]. Consequently the proposal's claim that it remains unbiased under MNAR is not established. The experiment tests empirical utility, not that claim.

The hidden clean training values are unavailable to the learner. Training uses only fixture-provided masked rows and labels. Anchors 0 and 1 remain observed by the fixture. Validation selection uses only clean, MCAR 20%, MCAR 40%, and anchor-dependent MAR cases; the reserved final mechanisms are not used for fitting, calibration, selection, or development. No target-batch adaptation is used.

## Prior art checked

- Mercaldo & Blume, “Missing data and prediction: the pattern submodel” (Biostatistics 2020): https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxy040 . It fits submodels per recurring missingness pattern using data from that pattern and shows predictive performance can remain strong under MNAR. This is the closest conceptual antecedent to pattern-specific experts; the proposed subsets are overlapping feature subsets rather than one model per exact mask pattern.
- Stempfle, Panahi & Johansson, “Sharing Pattern Submodels for Prediction with Missing Values” (AAAI 2023): https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26179 . It shares parameters between pattern-specific models via sparsity regularization. It is closer than the proposal acknowledges and weakens the claim that subset experts are a distinct primitive. A faithful SPSM reproduction is not a direct HGB control: its published method is a regularized linear/logistic architecture, and the paper's recurring-pattern regime differs from this fixture's near-unique random masks.
- Rockenschaub et al., “Robust prediction under missingness shifts” (2024): https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16484 . Its invariance claim is specifically for ignorable shifts where missingness depends only on observed covariates. The fixture's MAR validation condition is of that type; own-value MNAR is explicitly outside that guarantee, and the paper notes the Bayes predictor may change under non-ignorable shifts.
- Lee, Kang & Kim, “MIRRAMS: Learning Robust Tabular Models under Unseen Missingness Shifts” (2025): https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08280 . The proposal names this as its strongest prior comparator. The paper is a deep-learning method with MI-based conditions and its own objective; a tree adaptation would not be faithful. We therefore do not call the HGB controls a MIRRAMS reproduction.
- “Resilient Vision-Tabular Multimodal Learning under Modality Missingness” (2026): https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12031 . The source atom motivates stochastic modality dropout. This is cross-modal deep learning; random feature masking in HGB is only a constituent-style control, not a reproduction.

## Implementation and controls

`experiment.py` exposes `validation` and `final` modes. The candidate uses 40 deterministic subsets (seed-derived), 6 boosting iterations per expert, and validation-fitted temperature and normalized exponential loss weights. The subset list and all model settings are frozen in `preregistration.json`. Matched controls use the same aggregate 240 boosting iterations: (1) one native-NaN HGB with 240 iterations and (2) one HGB trained with additional random feature masking and 240 iterations. Candidate ablations remove validation weights (uniform weights), append mask indicators to experts, use anchor-only input, remove anchors from subsets, or force mean-imputed all-expert prediction. These controls isolate familiar model capacity and dropout components; none is claimed to be the theoretically strongest possible method.

No feature scaling is used, so there is no weight/standardization cancellation. All training filters are based only on observed/masked input availability. If a subset has too few complete rows, the script records the issue and uses its declared prior-free fallback (constant label prevalence) rather than accessing hidden values. Pooling always has an anchor-only fallback. Validation calibration uses labels only in validation mode. The prediction interface accepts only a masked feature matrix.

## Smallest falsification test and decision rule

Run the three fixture seeds in validation mode under the compute gate. The fixed primary metric is mean AP over the three non-clean validation masks (MCAR 20%, MCAR 40%, MAR), compared with the strongest measured matched control. A candidate requires a mean AP gain of at least 0.005 and no clean AP decrease greater than 0.01. Brier scores and per-condition results are secondary. This is a small pilot, not a novelty test or a confidence-interval claim. Validation does not contain own-value MNAR or grouped structural missingness; those are reserved for the parent-reviewed final run.

## Final evaluation (2026-11-16)

The parent-approved one-shot final run used the frozen candidate subset lists, candidate validation weights and temperature, and the same training recipe as validation. It read the fixture-provided `final_cases` only; predictions were generated from each masked matrix before labels were passed to the metric function. The fixture's actual grouped-condition keys are `block_mcar_2_8` and `block_mcar_8_14` (the pre-run approval record had a naming typo, corrected in that record after the run).

Across five non-clean final conditions and three seeds, candidate mean AP was **0.5553**, versus **0.8629** for native-NaN HGB and **0.8385** for random-mask augmentation. The candidate trailed native HGB on every paired seed/condition. Candidate clean AP was also lower than native HGB by 0.149, 0.114, and 0.174 for seeds 9401, 9403, and 9407. The final evaluation therefore does not alter the validation `no_gain` verdict.

A calibration detail: the validation script fitted separate weights and temperatures for the indicator, anchor-only, and no-anchor ablations, but only persisted the primary candidate's values. The final runner applied the primary candidate's frozen weights/temperature to those ablations. Thus their final scores are exploratory comparisons under a shared frozen calibration; they are not exact replications of each ablation's validation calibration. The primary candidate and the two HGB controls used their specified frozen final procedures. No parameters were tuned on final labels.
