# Does the layerwise intrinsic-dimension peak predict where critic-preference updates should be placed? A matched-budget window-placement test with an SFT collapse control

**Status:** unranked seed concept. No novelty claim; the prior-art review is bounded.

## 1. Hypothesis

For a fixed open-weight LM and a fixed AI-critic preference dataset, restricting preference-optimization (DPO) updates to a contiguous layer window anchored at the model's layerwise representation intrinsic-dimension (ID) peak recovers more of full-depth DPO's gain, on a critic-independent executable metric, than equally sized windows placed elsewhere, at equal trainable parameters and steps.

**Intervention:** the position of the trainable window along depth.
**Outcome:** held-out accuracy under a programmatic checker, expressed as recovery R(s) = (Acc_s − Acc_base) / (Acc_full − Acc_base).

**Proposed bridges (all conjecture):**
- **B1:** the ID peak marks the depth where preference-relevant features become available, so updates there are most efficient.
- **B2:** depth is treated as an ordered state axis along which the update is localized. This is a diluted use of atom 194 and tests nothing about ODE formulations.
- **B3:** the placement effect is specific to critic-preference objectives and not generic to fine-tuning. This is the bridge most likely to fail.

**Discrepancy:** the prior-art note describes source units (eye-tracking, video keyframes, MoE allocation) that do not match the units supplied here. I rely only on its cited links and its conclusion that the triplet specifies no interface.

## 3. Closest supplied prior art and the proposed difference

- **Component neighbor:** the abstraction-phase paper measures ID profiles and downstream transfer. The difference is that I intervene on where training updates go.
- **Pairwise neighbor (depth and compute):** Adaptive Computation Time ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08983)) and Neural ODEs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07366)) concern inference-time depth. This proposal concerns training-time placement and does not compete on inference FLOPs.
- **Strongest end-to-end competitor:** full-depth DPO and strong-teacher SFT as in the critical evaluation, plus RLAIF ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00267)). The note characterizes a latent-value account of RLAIF ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03000)) as linking judgment quality to representation directions. I have not verified that paper.
- **Uncertainty:** layer-selective fine-tuning and adapter-placement work is not in the supplied material and was not searched. It may already cover B1. The conjunction may well be covered.

## 4. Algorithm (invented design choices)

1. For each model m with L layers, compute ID(ℓ) on last-token residual activations for about 2,000 train-family prompts. Use a nearest-neighbor estimator, with PCA participation ratio as a robustness check. Set ℓ*_m to the argmax of the 3-layer-smoothed ID. If the two estimators disagree by more than L/8, mark the model "no clear peak" and exclude it.
2. Sample 4 base-model responses per train prompt. A frozen stronger critic labels pairwise preferences without access to the checker. The preference set is identical across arms.
3. Train rank-r LoRA with DPO on window [s, s+k), with k = L/4 and stride L/8, giving about 7 placements. Steps, learning-rate grid and seeds (3) are identical across placements.
4. Evaluate R(s) on held-out families.

## 5. Compatibility assumptions

- Homogeneous transformer layers, so parameters per window are equal.
- The critic's preferences agree with the checker above chance on train families. This is measured and never used for training.
- Full-depth DPO beats base by at least 3 accuracy points. Otherwise the run is declared **uninformative**, not failed. The critical evaluation's variability findings make this a real risk.

## 6. Phase-zero falsification experiment

**Data.** Programmatically generated tasks with executable checkers, for example templated multi-step arithmetic and string-transformation problems, in 12 template families. Eight families are for training and four are held out entirely. Fresh generation limits contamination. The checker is never shown to the critic or used in training.

**Arms.**
- **Baseline:** full-depth DPO with rank reduced so total trainable parameters match the window arms.
- **Simple heuristic baseline:** last-k window.
- **Geometry-free competitor:** window with the largest DPO gradient norm at initialization.
- **Positional control:** fixed relative-depth rule, chosen leave-one-model-out.
- **Negative control:** critic labels shuffled. Expected R ≈ 0 at every placement.
- **Ablation (collapse control for atom 250):** identical windows trained with SFT on critic-preferred responses.

**Kill step.** One model, 7 placements, 2 seeds, DPO only. If max R(s) − min R(s) < 0.10, stop, because placement does not matter enough to study.

**Full phase zero.** Four models from different families, selected so that ℓ*/L differs by at least 0.15 between at least two of them. If peaks coincide, the geometry-versus-position question is unidentifiable and is reported as such.

**Proposed preregistered rules.** Thresholds are my proposals, not derived from data.

- **P1, placement:** the ID-anchored window's R exceeds the mean R of the other placements by at least 0.15. The paired bootstrap 95% CI over items and seeds must exclude 0 in at least 3 of 4 models.
- **P2, geometry adds information:** the ID-anchored window beats both the gradient-norm window and the fixed relative-depth rule by at least 0.05 R on average. If either matches it, ID is redundant and the geometry claim fails.
- **P3, critic specificity:** the ID-anchored advantage under DPO exceeds that under SFT, with the CI excluding 0. If not, atom 250 is incidental. The result is then reported as a two-atom finding and the three-way conjunction is rejected.
- **Negative control:** if the shuffled-label arm shows |R| > 0.10 at any placement, the run is invalid.

## 7. Compute (estimate)

Assumptions: 1–2B-parameter models, about 5,000 preference pairs, sequences under 256 tokens, LoRA on one 24 GB GPU, about 30 GPU-minutes per run.

- **Kill step:** 14 runs, about 7 GPU-hours.
- **Full phase zero:** about 200 runs (4 models × 7 windows × 3 seeds × 2 objectives, plus controls), about 100 GPU-hours.
- **Critic labeling:** about 20,000 calls.
- **ID estimation:** under 1 GPU-hour.

Actual costs could differ by a factor of two or more.

## 8. Strongest objection and failure cases

**Objection.** The ID peak may be a proxy for "middle layers", and four models cannot separate geometry from position. P2 has low power, so a pass would be suggestive at most. B3 also has no source support. The abstraction-phase abstract says representations at the peak transfer downstream generally, which predicts a generic placement effect and therefore a P3 failure.

**Failure cases.**
- Full DPO does not beat base, as the critical evaluation's findings suggest can happen.
- ID profiles are flat, multi-peaked or estimator-dependent.
- Critic preferences track fluency instead of correctness.
- The window effect is driven by backward-path length and not by representation content.
- Results from templated tasks do not transfer to natural tasks.

## 9. Decision

**Investigate, kill step only.** Calibrated verdict: *plausibly distinct but unverified, with a high prior of collapsing to a pairwise (125 × 194) result*.

The geometry-to-placement interface is a real causal intervention with a cheap falsifier, which is why this is not an outright reject. The critic atom's contribution is the weakest bridge, and P3 exists to expose it. If P3 fails, the three-atom framing should be dropped, not rescued. If the kill step shows a flat R(s), reject the whole proposal.

Evidence that would raise the verdict: P2 passing with clearly separated peak positions across models, replicated with a second ID estimator.
