# Does the choice of curation scorer change group gaps in tamper localization? A recipe-matched selection experiment

## 1. Hypothesis

At a fixed retained-example count and training budget, selecting image-forensics training examples with a frozen VLM "quality" judge yields (a) more group-dependent retention and (b) a larger recipe-matched cross-group gap in tamper-localization accuracy on a held-out manipulation family than selecting with a content-suppressed low-level trace score, while trace-based selection does not lose held-out pixel AP relative to random selection.

## 2. Intervention and outcome

- **Intervention:** only the rule that picks a fixed-size training subset (VLM-judge score, trace score, or random).
- **Held fixed:** backbone, image count, augmentation, optimizer steps, and evaluation set.
- **Outcomes:** retention disparity across depicted demographic groups (Stage A, no training), then worst-pair group gap in pixel F1 plus overall pixel AP on an unseen manipulation family (Stage B).

Rationale faithfulness is out of scope, because the supplied material offers no evaluator independent of the VLM.

**Conjectured bridges (mine, not the sources'):**

1. A VLM curation scorer is an LLM-as-judge, so it may carry content-dependent preferences that correlate with depicted group.
2. A content-suppressed trace score is a less group-sensitive comparator.
3. A "bleached template" can be approximated in images by applying an identical tamper recipe to hosts from different groups.

**Proposed difference:** the scorer is treated as the audited object, with a retention-disparity first stage and a recipe-matched downstream gap. The notes found no paper making this intervention in a bounded check. That is weak evidence, and it may exist under other terminology.

## 5. Definitions

For scorer *s* and group *g*, let *r(s,g)* be the fraction of group-*g* members of matched tuples retained.

- **Retention disparity:** D_s = max_g r(s,g) − min_g r(s,g).
- **Localization gap:** Δ_s = max over group pairs of |m(s,g) − m(s,g′)|, where *m(s,g)* is the mean pixel F1 of the model trained under selection *s* on group-*g* members of held-out matched tuples.

Both max-statistics are biased upward under noise, so each is compared against a permuted-group-label null.

## 6. Compatibility assumptions

- A licensed image set with consented or annotated group labels exists. The supplied sources name none, so the choice must be justified separately.
- Tamper recipes can be applied identically across hosts.
- The trace scorer and VLM judge never see group labels and are not trained on evaluation hosts or the held-out family.
- The VLM judge is used directly in phase zero. Atom 63's distilled proxy is deferred, because distillation adds a second confound.

## 7. Phase-zero falsification experiment

**Dataset construction (benign, defensive):**

- Take authentic hosts from G = 4 groups.
- Define about 3,000 recipes, each fixing the operation (copy-move, object splice, or background inpainting), mask geometry in normalized coordinates, a non-person payload, and a post-processing seed (resize, JPEG quality).
- Apply each recipe to one host per group, giving about 12,000 tampered images in matched tuples.
- No face synthesis or identity alteration is used.

**Held-out scheme:**

- Hosts are disjoint across scorer-fitting, selection-pool, and test splits.
- One manipulation family (background inpainting) is entirely absent from the pool and scorer fitting, and forms the test set.
- Test payloads are unseen.
- Group labels are used only for metrics and for the ablation below.

**Conditions (each retains 50% of the pool, stratified by manipulation family, with the same steps):**

| Condition | Selection rule | Role |
|---|---|---|
| R | Random | Baseline and randomized control |
| V | Top-k by a frozen VLM rating of "usefulness as a forensic training example", from a fixed prompt on the image only | Treatment under suspicion |
| T | Top-k by trace score: in-mask versus out-of-mask contrast of a frozen high-pass residual detector fitted on disjoint data | Comparator |
| V-quota | V's ranking with group quotas matched to R | Ablation separating composition effects from which-examples effects |

The negative control is pseudo-groups formed by permuting labels within tuples.

**Stage A (inference only).** Compute D_V, D_T and the permutation null. Pass requires both:

- D_V exceeds the 95th percentile of the null.
- The tuple-bootstrap 95% CI of D_V − D_T lies above zero.

Failing Stage A means the mechanism has no first stage, and the work stops.

**Stage B (only if A passes).** Train one small RGB-plus-residual segmentation backbone per condition with 3 seeds. Pass requires both:

- The paired-bootstrap 95% CI over recipes, pooled across seeds, of Δ_V − Δ_T lies above zero.
- AP_T ≥ AP_R − 1.0 pixel-AP point on the held-out family. This margin is a proposed placeholder, to be frozen before unblinding.

Failing either part falsifies the hypothesis. If V-quota removes the gap, the finding reduces to "stratify by group", which is an incremental result.

## 8. Compute (estimate)

- **Stage A:** about 12,000 VLM inferences plus residual scoring, roughly 5–10 GPU-hours on one 24 GB GPU.
- **Stage B:** 4 conditions × 3 seeds, at an assumed 1–2 GPU-hours per run for a small segmentation model at 512 px, roughly 12–24 GPU-hours.

These figures assume no M-LLM fine-tuning. A ForgerySleuth-scale model would cost far more and is not proposed here.

## 9. Strongest objection and failure cases

**Strongest objection:** recipe matching is not a true counterfactual. Group membership in real photographs correlates with camera, lighting, exposure, and compression history, and these directly alter low-level residuals. The trace score may therefore be more group-dependent than the VLM score, reversing the hypothesis. That reversal would be informative, but it undermines bridge 2.

**Other failure cases:**

- Top-k trace selection may keep easy, family-specific artifacts and fail the AP guardrail. The supplied notes flag this shortcut risk.
- Coarse or perceived group labels can make the gaps uninterpretable.
- A small-backbone result may not transfer to M-LLM forensic systems of the atom-93 type.
- A single VLM judge and prompt may give idiosyncratic results. A second judge is needed before generalizing.
- The mechanics of inpainting may differ from the pooled families in ways that swamp selection effects.

## 10. Decision

**Investigate, Stage A only, with Stage B gated on it.**

The conjunction has one plausible non-trivial interface: the curation scorer is a judge whose retention decisions can be audited counterfactually, with the forensic trace signal as a content-suppressed comparator. Stage A tests whether that interface exists at all for a few GPU-hours.

This is not a claim of novelty or of expected success. If Stage A fails, the verdict becomes **reject** as an unsupported collage. If only V-quota explains the effect, the verdict becomes **incremental**.
