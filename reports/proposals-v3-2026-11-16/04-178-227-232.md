# Background-Subspace-Projected Recovery Steering from Branch-Matched Agent Trajectories: A Phase-Zero Test

**Status:** unranked seed combination (atoms 178, 227, 232). This is a research question with a kill test, not a claimed method or a novelty claim.

## 1. Hypothesis

For a frozen open-weight agent in a benign simulated tool environment, a steering vector estimated from the model's own failed-versus-recovered continuations after a tool error gives higher held-out error-recovery at a fixed retention budget when its component in the top-*r* principal subspace of unrelated-task activations is removed (norm restored) than the unprojected mean-difference vector does.

**Proposed bridges (all conjectural):**

- **B1:** Branch-matched continuations from the same error state give a usable contrastive pair.
- **B2:** Their mean activation difference causally raises recovery.
- **B3:** Off-target damage from steering is concentrated in the high-variance subspace of unrelated-task activations, so projecting it out improves the trade-off. B3 could plausibly be false in the opposite direction, if the behaviorally effective component lies inside that subspace.

**Scope limit:** atom 232 contributes only the data source here. No training occurs, so this does not test "self-improvement" or durable learning.

## 3. Intervention and outcome

**Contrast extraction (B1).** At each training error observation, sample 8 continuations from the identical prefix and label each by the environment's programmatic success check. For branch points with at least one success and one failure, teacher-force both next actions and compute at layer *L*:

d_i = mean over success-action tokens of h_L − mean over failure-action tokens of h_L, and v = meanᵢ d_i

**Projection (B3).** Collect centered layer-*L* activations X from background tasks (error-free tool use and general prompts). Take X = UΣVᵀ with top-*r* right singular vectors V_r, then:

v⊥ = (I − V_r V_rᵀ) v, rescaled so that ‖v⊥‖ = ‖v‖

**Steering.** Add α·v (or α·v⊥) to h_L at every generated position after an error observation.

**Primary outcome.** Held-out recovery rate is the share of error episodes that the environment checker marks solved within the step budget. It is measured at the largest α whose dev-set retention drop is at most 2 points absolute.

## 4. Closest supplied prior art and exact difference

| Role | Source | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| Component baseline | [ActAdd](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10248), [RepE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405) | The unprojected v is essentially their recipe applied to agent branch points. That arm is the baseline, not the contribution. |
| Pairwise neighbor | [LEACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819) | LEACE erases a concept by a closed-form subspace intervention. Here a projection restricts a steering vector to spare unrelated activations. |
| Strongest end-to-end competitor | [Trajectory-informed memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10600) | It uses the same failure/success signal but delivers it as retrieved prompt tips. Here the signal is delivered with zero added context tokens. |

**Uncertainty.** The bounded review found no supplied source doing exactly this, which is weak evidence. Covariance-aware or orthogonalized steering may exist under other terminology, and v⊥ is close kin to whitened and LDA-style directions. A fuller search must precede any claim of difference.

## 5. Compatibility assumptions

- White-box access to residual activations, and a deterministic simulated environment with a programmatic checker.
- Base recovery rate between 20% and 80% on dev. Otherwise there is no contrast data or no headroom, and difficulty must be retuned before the preregistration freeze.
- Action tokens are localizable for teacher forcing.
- **SVD diagnostic gates, computed before any evaluation:**
  - If the top singular vector of the stacked d_i explains under 20% of their energy, the single-direction assumption (B2) is unsupported. Record the gate failure.
  - If ‖V_rᵀv‖²/‖v‖² is under 5% for all preregistered *r*, the projection is a no-op and the test is inconclusive, not a pass.

## 6. Phase-zero falsification experiment

**Dataset construction.** Use about 40 synthetic task templates (file lookup, unit conversion, record query) with mock tools. In error episodes the first obvious call returns an injected error and success requires an alternative tool or argument. Three control sets guard against the vector meaning only "do something different":

- **Retry-correct tasks:** the error is transient and repeating the call is the right action.
- **Error-free tool tasks:** these detect needless abandonment of a working tool.
- **A small general QA set.**

**Held-out scheme.**

- Split by task template and by error family. Fit on two families (for example not-found and permission-denied) and test on two unseen families (for example schema-mismatch and quota) with unseen tool names.
- Draw background activations X, the dev-retention set and the test-retention set from disjoint templates, so the projection is not fitted to the tasks it is scored on.
- Select *L*, *r* ∈ {8, 32, 128} and α on dev only, then freeze them.
- The evaluator is the environment checker, never the model or a probe.

**Arms.** All use the same model, decoding, step and tool budget, and matched ‖v‖.

1. No steering.
2. **Named baseline:** unprojected mean-difference v.
3. **Proposed:** v⊥.
4. **Negative control:** random direction.
5. **Negative control:** v from success/failure-shuffled labels, renormalized.
6. **Ablation:** project out a random *r*-dimensional subspace instead of V_r.
7. **Ablation:** project out the bottom-*r* background subspace.
8. **Competitor:** a prompt tip distilled from the same training trajectories, with its token count reported. Arms 1–7 use zero added context.

**Metrics.** Primary is held-out recovery at the 2-point retention budget. Secondary are retry-correct accuracy, error-free task success, QA accuracy and the full recovery–retention curve over α.

**Proposed preregistered rule (thresholds are proposals).**

- **Pass** requires all three of:
  - arm 3 exceeds arm 2 by at least 5 points absolute on the primary metric, with a paired-bootstrap 95% CI lower bound above 0 over held-out episodes;
  - arm 3 exceeds arms 4 and 5 by at least 10 points;
  - arm 3 exceeds arms 6 and 7 by at least 3 points, so the gain is specific to the background principal subspace.
- **Fail (B2):** arm 2 does not beat arms 4 and 5. There is then no transferable recovery direction.
- **Fail (B3):** arm 3 is no better than arm 2, or no better than arm 6.
- **Reported regardless:** if arm 8 dominates arm 3 on both recovery and retention, any pass is of mechanistic interest only.

## 7. Compute (estimate)

**Assumptions:** one 1–4B-parameter open-weight instruct model, a single 24 GB GPU and at most 8 steps per episode.

- **Extraction:** about 600 branch points × 8 continuations.
- **Evaluation:** about 400 test and 300 dev episodes × 8 arms × up to 6 α values.
- **SVD:** about 20k background activations, which is negligible.

**Estimate:** 15–30 GPU-hours, dominated by evaluation rollouts. No fine-tuning is involved.

## 8. Strongest objection and failure cases

**Strongest objection.** This is ActAdd plus a projection, with trajectories as incidental data. Any gain may come from changing the effective steering strength per direction, not from "protecting" unrelated computation. Arms 6 and 7 and matched norms address this only partly.

**Failure cases.**

- The recovery direction lies largely inside the top background subspace, so projection destroys it.
- v encodes surface features of the training error families or tool names and does not transfer.
- v encodes generic action-switching, which would show as collapsed retry-correct accuracy.
- Branch-matched success labels reflect sampling luck, giving a noisy v.
- Layer and context sensitivity, as cautioned by the truth-direction abstract, makes the dev-selected *L* fail on held-out families.
- Results in a synthetic environment may not carry to realistic agent benchmarks.

## 9. Invented design choices (not in the atoms)

Branch-matched sampling, action-token averaging, the projection form, norm restoration, the retention budget, all thresholds, the task families and the SVD gates.

## 10. Decision

**Investigate at phase-zero only. Calibrated verdict: plausibly distinct but unverified, and likely incremental.**

- **Why not reject:** the causal interface is explicit (B1–B3), and a cheap experiment with specific controls can break each bridge.
- **Why not more:** the conjunction's contribution rests entirely on B3. Atom 232 is used only as a data source. Prompt-tip memory is a strong and simpler competitor.
- **If the test fails:** a B3 failure with B2 holding reduces the idea to known contrastive steering, and it should be dropped. A B2 failure means the trajectory-contrast direction itself is unsupported in this setting.
