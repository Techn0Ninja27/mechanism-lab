# Does surprise-drift triage pick the right tail facts to edit back into a synthetic-data generator? A one-transition transmission test under replace-regime recursive fine-tuning

## 1. Hypothesis

In a replace-regime recursive fine-tuning chain, spending a fixed budget of K rank-one edits on the generator, on tail facts chosen by frequency-normalized surprise drift, yields higher held-out-template tail-fact recall in the next-generation student than the same K edits spent on randomly chosen or currently-wrong facts.

## 3. Proposed interface (my conjecture, not source-backed)

The causal chain is: surprise drift selects facts, an edit changes the generator's weights, the generator's samples change, and the student's recall changes.

The unsupported link is the third. Collapse is plausibly a sampling-side loss, because tail subjects are rarely emitted. Editing repairs storage. Whether a stored repair reaches the synthetic corpus is unknown, and it is the main thing this test measures.

The motivating constraint is a publisher who controls generator weights but not the downstream training corpus. This is the only setting in which editing is not trivially dominated by adding the verified facts as text.

The triage score for fact *i* = (subject sᵢ, relation r, object oᵢ) at generation *t* uses a cloze template q:

dᵢᵗ = [NLLₜ(oᵢ | q(sᵢ)) − NLLₜ(oᵢ | q(∅))] − [NLL₀(oᵢ | q(sᵢ)) − NLL₀(oᵢ | q(∅))]

Here q(∅) replaces the subject with a neutral placeholder. The inner difference removes object-frequency effects, and subtracting generation 0 isolates drift. The top-K facts by dᵢᵗ are edited.

## 4. Intervention and outcome

- **Intervention:** the rule that selects which K verified tail facts receive ROME-style edits in the generation-t generator, before it emits the corpus for generation t+1.
- **Primary outcome:** exact-match recall of tail-fact objects by the generation-t+1 student on templates never seen in training, generation, selection or editing.
- **Secondary outcomes:**
  - transmission rate on the edited facts;
  - recall on never-edited facts;
  - held-out real-corpus perplexity;
  - distinct-n of the synthetic corpus;
  - false-positive rate, meaning the student predicts an edited object for unedited subjects of the same relation.

## 6. Compatibility assumptions (each can fail)

1. ROME-style edits succeed on a small GPT-2 fine-tuned on fictional multi-token entities. This is checked in the generator before anything else.
2. K sequential edits do not wreck the generator. The Butterfly Effect note says even a few edits can, so K is small, and each student is trained fresh from the base checkpoint so that weight damage does not compound.
3. A verified fact list exists. This restricts scope to curated knowledge.

## 7. Phase-zero falsification experiment

**Dataset.** Build a template-generated corpus about roughly 2,000 fictional entities with one attribute each, which avoids pretraining contamination and keeps the task benign. Mention frequency is Zipfian, with tail entities in 1–3 documents. Templates are split into four disjoint sets: training, selection (q), edit-request and evaluation.

**Chain.** Generation 0 is GPT-2-small fine-tuned on the real corpus. Each generation samples an equal-size synthetic corpus with top-p sampling from generic document prefixes. The next student is fine-tuned from the base checkpoint on that corpus only. A dev chain with separate seeds picks t\*, the first generation where tail recall is at or below 50% of generation 0, and fixes K at 50. The test then runs one transition, t\* to t\*+1, over 5 seeds.

**Held-out scheme.**
- Evaluation templates are disjoint from all other uses.
- 20% of tail entities are never edit-eligible, to measure collateral loss.
- No generated text is used for evaluation.
- All thresholds are frozen on the dev chain.

**Arms.** K, edit order policy, generator sampling budget and student training steps are identical across arms.

| Arm | Description |
|---|---|
| A | No-op (skip baseline) |
| B | Random K eligible tail facts |
| C | K random among currently argmax-wrong facts (named strongest simple baseline) |
| D | Top-K by raw NLLₜ (ablation: no normalization, no drift) |
| E | Top-K by dᵢᵗ (proposed) |
| F | No edits; the K verified fact sentences are appended to the student corpus (replay reference, needs corpus control) |
| G | Negative control: arm E's selection, but edited to shuffled objects |

**Proposed preregistered rules.**
- **Gate 0 (edit efficacy):** generator-side edit success on edit-request templates is at least 80% in arm B. If not, stop and report "incompatible."
- **Gate 1 (transmission):** student recall on arm B's edited facts exceeds arm A's recall on the same facts by at least 10 absolute points, with the 95% paired bootstrap interval over facts and seeds excluding 0. If this fails, reject the interface, because selection is then moot.
- **Gate 2 (selection):** arm E exceeds both B and C on eligible-tail recall by at least 3 points, with the interval excluding 0. Its non-edited-fact recall must be no more than 2 points below arm A, and its false-positive rate must not exceed arm B's.
- **Arm G check:** true-object recall on the selected facts must not rise above arm A. If it does, the gains are perturbation artifacts.
- **Arm D reading:** if E does not beat D, the normalization and drift terms add nothing over raw NLL.
- **Practical relevance:** report E's gain as a fraction of F's. Below 25%, the result is marked as mattering only where the corpus cannot be controlled.

## 8. Compute (estimate)

The estimate is roughly 25–40 GPU-hours on a single 24 GB GPU. It assumes a ~2M-token corpus, about 30 minutes per fine-tune-plus-generate cycle on the 124M-parameter model, about 45 cycles (dev chain plus 7 arms × 5 seeds), and seconds per edit. None of these figures is measured.

## 9. Strongest objection and failure cases

**Strongest objection.** With a verified fact list in hand, replay (arm F) is simpler and probably better. Editing also repairs storage, while the loss is in sampling: a generator that rarely emits a tail subject will not transmit its repaired attribute.

Drift triage may make this worse by preferentially picking the facts whose subjects have vanished from samples, which are the least transmissible. In that case E would lose to B. I consider failure at Gate 1 a likely outcome.

**Other failure cases.**
- dᵢᵗ may be nearly rank-equivalent to argmax-wrong, so E ≈ C.
- Edits may raise object frequency indiscriminately, inflating recall. The false-positive metric and arm G are meant to catch this.
- Fictional-entity and template results may not transfer to natural corpora, larger models or accumulation regimes.
- One transition says nothing about long-run dynamics.
- A positive result would show a measured interaction in this setup only; it would not show that editing prevents collapse.

## 10. Decision

**Investigate, phase zero only; otherwise reject.**

- The broad version, a perplexity-triggered edit controller, is rejected as near-covered by the Butterfly Effect work per the prior-art note.
- The retained question has a real but unverified causal interface: whether edits transmit through sampling, and whether surprise drift selects transmissible repairs. It also has a cheap kill test with a matched edit count.
- If Gate 1 fails, the conjunction should be recorded as incoherent for this purpose. If Gate 1 passes and Gate 2 fails, only the two-atom finding survives, and atom 59 contributes nothing.

**Invented design choices (not in the sources):** the drift score, the fictional corpus, the publisher-control motivation, fresh-from-base students, K = 50, and all thresholds.
